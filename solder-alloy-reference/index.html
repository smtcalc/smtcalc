Solder Alloy Reference

RoHS-compliant lead-free alloys, compared side by side. Filter by category or process, sort any column, and select up to three alloys for a direct comparison. All data from published alloy specifications.

01: Temperature columns

Solidus

The temperature at which the alloy begins to melt. Below this point the solder is fully solid. In a reflow oven, time above solidus is what closes the joint, so a lower solidus gives more margin if your oven has poor thermal uniformity.

Liquidus

The temperature at which the alloy is fully liquid. Reflow peak must exceed liquidus to achieve wetting. A higher liquidus means a hotter oven and more thermal stress on components.

Pasty range

The gap between solidus and liquidus. In this semi-solid state the alloy is sensitive to movement. A wide range increases the risk of disturbed joints if the board moves during cooling.

02: Process columns

Reflow peak

Recommended peak temperature for convection reflow, typically set 15-30°C above liquidus to ensure complete wetting without exceeding component ratings. The upper limit is usually driven by the most temperature-sensitive component, not the alloy.

Wave / Selective

Recommended bath temperature for wave or selective soldering. Wave baths run hotter than reflow peaks because contact time is short. N/A means the alloy is not recommended for wave, usually brittleness, narrow window, or Pb-contamination risk.

03: Alloy categories

Standard SAC

Sn-Ag-Cu alloys with 3.0-4.0% silver, liquidus around 217-221°C. The mainstream choice for most RoHS assembly; SAC305 is the de facto standard. Higher silver improves thermal fatigue life but increases cost.

Low / Ag-Free

Reduced or zero silver for lower material cost, liquidus typically 217-228°C. Mechanical and thermal-cycling performance is generally lower than standard SAC. Good for cost-driven consumer lines.

Low-Temp

Bismuth-tin alloys, liquidus around 138-141°C, enabling reflow below 200°C. Critical risk: any lead contamination forms a Bi-Pb ternary eutectic at 96°C, causing field failures. Strict BOM control is mandatory.

High-Rel

Multi-element alloys for extended thermal cycling (-40 to +150°C per AEC-Q100 Grade 0). Bi, Sb, Ni additions strengthen the matrix against creep. More expensive; often need supplier-specific qualification.
